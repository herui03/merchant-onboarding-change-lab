Merchant Onboarding Policy Change Lab

Business Analyst & UAT case study — an independent, synthetic workflow prototype.

A fictional payment provider, Fernhill Payments (invented), onboards corporate merchants. An onboarding specialist prepares an application with document metadata. A risk reviewer inspects an immutable submitted revision against the evidence policy, then requests information, approves or rejects it.

The business change (CR-001): from 2026-07-01, evidence policy v2 requires a service-scope statement for designated deferred-delivery products (SUBSCRIPTION, FUTURE_DELIVERY) in the elevated volume bands V3/V4. The statement must be issued within 90 days before the revision's submission date. Approved v1 cases are grandfathered. In-flight cases follow an explicit migration rule, and a stale reviewer action fails.

What is real here

  • A working state machine with immutable revisions and decisions.
  • Server-side role and self-review checks, idempotent requests and stale-action refusal.
  • A policy engine that matches 24 hand-authored examples written before the code.
  • Automated service tests and a Chromium browser run that produced the evidence.

What is invented

  • Policies, thresholds, companies, people and documents. The identifiers are SYN-….
  • Business context and assumptions. They were authored, not gathered in stakeholder interviews.
  • "Acting as" is a lab switch, not authentication.

What has not happened

  • External UAT and business signoff. Only developer acceptance checks exist.
  • Any real KYC, legal or compliance assessment.
  • Deployment. The app binds to 127.0.0.1 only.
